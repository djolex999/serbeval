# Normative Serbian rulings: adverbial phrases, numeral agreement, fixed expressions

Overall caveat: none of the four primary normative works (Pravopis MS 2010/2017, Klajn's *Rečnik jezičkih nedoumica* (RJN), Piper & Klajn's *Normativna gramatika* 2013, Stanojčić & Popović) is available as searchable full text on the open web. A pdfcoffee copy of RJN returned HTTP 403. So **no tačka, page, or entry text from a primary source was verified directly** in this session. Everything cited below is secondary unless marked otherwise. One finding comes close to primary: Klajn, the Pravopis 2010 reviewer, quoted in a 2011 newspaper interview (Q4).

## 1. „na brzinu“ vs „nabrzinu“

### Takeaway
Every Serbian-oriented source found says to write it apart, „na brzinu“, and treats „nabrzinu“ as wrong. None of them cites a Pravopis tačka or rečnik entry, so the ruling is secondary only. Confidence that „na brzinu“ is the standard form: **medium-high**. Confidence in any specific Pravopis citation: **none found**.

### Cited Findings
- SECONDARY: 011info's spelling page gives the verdict „Ispravno je na brzinu.“ It cites no source. — [011info](https://www.011info.com/pravopis-srpskog-jezika/spojeno-ili-odvojeno/na-brzinu-ili-nabrzinu)
- SECONDARY: Elektronska knjiga's "Pravopis" site says „Pravilno je pisati „na brzinu“.“ It names no edition, tačka, or author. — [pravopis.elektronskaknjiga.com](https://pravopis.elektronskaknjiga.com/na-brzinu-ili-nabrzinu)
- SECONDARY: Espreso's popular spelling lesson lists it as written apart (taken from search snippets, not fetched). — [Espreso](https://www.espreso.co.rs/vesti/drustvo/1138753/evo-sta-se-pise-spojeno-a-sta-odvojeno)
- SECONDARY, divergent, Bosnian-oriented: kakosepise.info claims a meaning split, „nabrzinu“ = „brzo“ vs „na brzinu“. No Serbian source supports this. — [kakosepise.info](https://kakosepise.info/nabrzinu-ili-na-brzinu.html)
- Academic, background only: Čutura notes that changes to the Pravopis conventions moved some units between "prilog" and "prilošk(i) izraz". She gives no example for „na brzinu“. — [Čutura, Sintaksa priloških izraza (PDF)](https://www.andricevinstitut.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/Илијана-Чутура-СИНТАКСА-ПРИЛОШКИХ-ИЗРАЗА-Одј.-за-српски-језик.pdf)

### Inferences
- „na brzinu“ fits the general Pravopis principle that a preposition plus a noun case form keeps the two words apart when it works as an adverbial phrase („na primer“, „na vreme“). This is an inference from the general rule, not a verified entry.

### Gaps
- Not verified whether the Pravopis 2010/2017 pravopisni rečnik has an entry „brzina“ / „na brzinu“, or under which tačka (the section on "pisanje priloga i priloških izraza").
- Not verified whether Klajn's RJN has an entry. It could not be accessed.

## 2. „do kasno (uveče / u noć)“ vs „do kasna“

### Takeaway
No primary normative ruling was found. The only sources were a stylistics paper that treats both forms as attested variants, and a popular site that promotes a fused adverb „докасно“. Neither settles which is standard. Confidence on any normative verdict: **low**. Do not mark either form as an error on current evidence.

### Cited Findings
- SECONDARY (popular site, no author or citation): „Језички дневник“ says the adverb „касно“ joins „до“ into „докасно“, glossed as "дуго, до касно у ноћ". Its own gloss uses „до касно у ноћ“ written apart. It does not mention „до касна“ and cites neither Pravopis nor Klajn. — [Језички дневник, „До касно или докасно“ (5 Aug 2020)](https://jezickidnevnik.rs/kako-se-pise/)
- ACADEMIC (stylistics, descriptive not normative): a paper on Svetlana Velmar-Janković's novel *Бездно* gives „до касна“ alongside the „до касно“ variant as stylistic variation (from the abstract via search). — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/378527405_Stilske_mikrostrukture_u_jeziku_romana_'Bezdno'_Svetlane_Velmar-Jankovic)
- Usage: media texts widely use „од раног јутра до касно у ноћ“ (search-summary observation, not normative).

### Inferences
- „до касна“ looks like an older, adverb-in-genitive type, like „до скора“ or „од давнина“. „до касно у ноћ“ is the dominant modern usage. If a normative work comments, the likely shape is "both accepted, with a stylistic note". This is unverified, so treat any benchmark item that rejects one form as **unsafe**.

### Gaps
- Klajn RJN entries „касно“ / „до“: not accessible. No quotation found online.
- Pravopis 2010 rečnik entries „докасно“ / „до касна“: not verified.
- No Odbor za standardizaciju odluka or Politika / Jezik danas column on this phrase was found.

## 3. Agreement: „učestvovalo je dvadeset dva učenika“ vs „učestvovala su dvadeset dva učenika“

### Takeaway
Secondary school-grammar sources give the plural (neuter plural for masculine and neuter nouns) as the rule with 2-4 and with compounds ending in 2-4: „Došla su dvadeset dva dečaka“. Singular neuter („došlo je“) is the rule from „pet“ upward. None of the sources found calls singular neuter with 2-4 wrong, but none endorses it either. The primary grammars (Piper & Klajn 2013, Stanojčić & Popović) could not be checked. Confidence that the plural is the normative form: **medium**. Confidence on whether singular neuter is outright wrong: **low**.

### Cited Findings
- SECONDARY (school grammar site, no source cited): with dva/tri/četiri and compounds ending in 2/3/4, the noun takes the genitive singular and the verb the plural, neuter plural for masculine and neuter nouns. Its example is „Došla su dvadeset dva dečaka.“ Singular neuter is given for numbers from pet up: „Došlo je dvadeset sedam dečaka.“ — [opsteobrazovanje.in.rs, Kongruencija](https://www.opsteobrazovanje.in.rs/srpski-jezik/gramatika/kongruencija/)
- OLDER GRAMMAR (historical, not current norm): Ž. Simić's *Српска граматика* says that with два, оба, три, четири „prirok dolazi u množinu“. — [Викизворник, Симић, гл. 6](https://sr.wikisource.org/sr/%D0%A1%D1%80%D0%BF%D1%81%D0%BA%D0%B0_%D0%B3%D1%80%D0%B0%D0%BC%D0%B0%D1%82%D0%B8%D0%BA%D0%B0_(%D0%96._%D0%A1%D0%B8%D0%BC%D0%B8%D1%9B)/%D0%9F%D0%BE%D0%B3%D0%BB%D0%B0%D0%B2%D1%99%D0%B5_6)
- SECONDARY (language column, Dnevnik „Језикоманија“, N. Mirković, 2026): covers only collective numerals (двоје, троје). Their predicate is singular neuter: „Двоје људи је дошло“. Agreement by meaning in the plural is common in speech, but the singular is safer in formal language. It does not cover dva/tri/četiri or „dvadeset dva“. — [Dnevnik](https://www.dnevnik.rs/kultura/najnovije-vesti/jezikomanija-jednina-ili-mnozina-2026-08-28)
- SECONDARY (school/pismenica): Piper & Klajn's grammar is reported to allow both -а and -и participles with masculine numeral nouns („Двојица су одустала / одустали“). That concerns двојица-type numerals, not два. — [Писменица, Мушколични бројеви](https://www.pismenica.rs/muskolicni-brojevi/)
- SECONDARY (Croatian school material, different norm, contrast only): "Glagol je uvijek u množini" after dva/tri/četiri. — [skole.hr PDF](https://web-arhiva.skole.hr/ss-donji-miholjac/upload/ss-donji-miholjac/images/static3/1795/File/Jezik.pdf)

### Inferences
- In an item built on the predicate agreement, „su učestvovala dvadeset dva učenika“ is the safe gold answer. „je učestvovalo dvadeset dva učenika“ cannot be labeled a normative error without the Piper & Klajn or Stanojčić & Popović text. Descriptively, singular neuter with 2-4 is widespread, especially when the predicate comes first and the subject is a quantified expression. Some grammars may tolerate it, but this is **unverified**.
- If the item asks the model to pick the only correct form, it is risky. Recommend human review or rewording the item to test the plural vs collective-numeral contrast instead.

### Gaps
- Piper & Klajn, *Normativna gramatika srpskog jezika* (MS 2013): the section on agreement with "paukalni" numerals was not accessible. No tačka or page could be cited.
- Stanojčić & Popović, *Gramatika srpskog jezika* (ZUNS): not accessible.
- Klajn RJN entries „dva“ / „slaganje“: not accessible.

## 4. „hteo-ne hteo“ / „hteo ne hteo“ / „hteo – ne hteo“

### Takeaway
Pravopis MS 2010 apparently prescribes a joining mark for „hteo ne hteo“ but writes „radio ne radio“ without one. Klajn, the book's reviewer, publicly criticized this inconsistency. Sources disagree on whether the mark is the crtica („hteo-ne hteo“) or the spaced crta („hteo – ne hteo“). Confidence that Pravopis 2010 uses *some* joining mark for this phrase: **medium** (Klajn's interview). Confidence about which mark: **low**. Not verified whether the 2017 edition changed it.

### Cited Findings
- NEAR-PRIMARY (Klajn quoted in Novosti, Ivana Mićević, 8 Oct 2011): Klajn, as reviewer of the new 2010 Matica srpska Pravopis, lists as an unnecessary doublet the rule „da se sa crtom piše „hteo ne hteo“ a bez crte „radio ne radio““. He asks for such details to be unified in the next edition. The article mixes the terms crta and crtica. — [Novosti, „Pravopis: Mini suknja duža za crtu“](https://www.novosti.rs/vesti/naslovna/aktuelno.69.html:348370-pravopis)
- SECONDARY (tabanovic.com, "Novosti u novom Pravopisu", item 5 "Dubleti"): says that under the new Pravopis „hteo-ne hteo“ is written with a hyphen, while „radio ne radio“ has none. No tačka is cited. — [tabanovic.com](https://www.tabanovic.com/1.pravopis.htm)
- SECONDARY (from search snippet, page not verified): one guide says doubled verb forms take a hyphen („hoću-neću“), while multi-word parts take a spaced dash, giving „hteo – ne hteo“ as its example. — [kakosepise.net](https://kakosepise.net/tag/teo-ne-hteo/)
- SECONDARY (glossary): the crtica joins parts of polusloženice, with the example „хоће-неће“. — [Lingvistički termini, цртица](https://www.lingvistickitermini.rs/pojmovnik/crtica/)

### Inferences
- The likely reconciliation: Pravopis 2010 joins two-word members with a spaced crta, and „ne hteo“ is two words, which gives „hteo – ne hteo“. Klajn's word „crta“ fits this. Single-word pairs such as „hoće-neće“ and „milom-silom“ take the crtica. This is plausible but unverified against the Pravopis text.
- For the benchmark: both „hteo-ne hteo“ and „hteo – ne hteo“ have some normative backing. The plain unpunctuated „hteo ne hteo“ is the form the 2010 Pravopis seems to reject, judging by Klajn's complaint. Do not build an item whose gold depends on crtica vs crta.

### Gaps
- The exact Pravopis 2010/2017 tačka (crtica/crta, polusloženice) and the rečnik entry for „hteti“ / „hteo ne hteo“ were not found online.
- Not verified whether the 2017 izmenjeno i dopunjeno izdanje changed this, as Klajn asked.
- Not verified whether „milom-silom“ appears explicitly in Pravopis 2010. Only the „хоће-неће“ analogy came from a secondary glossary.
